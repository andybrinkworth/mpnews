- **eBay has started inserting individual Depop listings directly into its own search results, with no "Sponsored" label, placing them ahead of the paid ads its own sellers buy (reported 12 November).** Testing by Value Added Resource found a Depop item taking the No. 1 spot in an eBay Android app search — a position previously reserved for eBay's own cost-per-click Promoted Listings Priority ads — carrying a Depop logo and "View on Depop" text that opens an eBay-hosted preview before sending the buyer off-platform to complete the purchase on Depop. In the tested case, the eBay-listed price was $3.45 higher than the equivalent Depop listing — almost exactly Depop's own Marketplace Fee, which eBay's displayed price includes up front while Depop only adds it at checkout, the subject of a separate pending US class action over Depop's "drip pricing." At least one eBay seller has reported a Depop listing directly competing with her own eBay listing for the same search term. eBay's own policies bar independent sellers from directing buyers off-platform, while eBay itself is now doing exactly that for Depop, which it acquired for $1.4bn in a deal that closed in July; Depop's US GMV, active users and items sold all more than doubled year-on-year on a pro forma basis in eBay's Q3, adding almost three points to eBay's consolidated GMV growth. Why it matters: this is a concrete, testable example of a marketplace blending a newly-acquired platform into its core search ahead of its own paying sellers' ads — exactly the kind of self-preferencing question regulators have been asking about Amazon's ranking mechanics, but self-inflicted here rather than imposed by a rival. For a commission marketplace, it's a sharp reminder that how search and ranking treat an owned-but-separate channel versus the suppliers paying for visibility is a trust question worth settling deliberately, not discovering after sellers notice.

- **A CarParts.com marketplace partner has threatened to terminate the company's seller relationship over its new CarParts Connect marketplace — which is built on Mirakl, the same platform underpinning Booker Marketplace (disclosed in CarParts.com's Q3 2026 filing, reported 10 November).** CarParts.com's 10-Q discloses that in October it received a letter from an unnamed "large third-party marketplace partner" — not named in the filing or in available reporting — saying its planned CarParts Connect launch creates a conflict of interest, and that the partner intends to end CarParts.com's seller relationship once it launches. CarParts Connect, announced 22 October and due to launch in December, is a Mirakl-powered marketplace and ad network open to US third-party sellers of new, authentic automotive and adjacent goods (accessories, electronics, travel, pet, outdoor), charging a generally 15% commission with no upfront or subscription fees and advertising "no exclusivity." eBay and Amazon together made up 30.3% of CarParts.com's revenue in the first nine months of 2026 (about $120.3m, down from 33.9% a year earlier), against overall revenue of $396.9m (down 7.1%) and a $9.3m net loss; CarParts.com says it expects "weeks and months" of talks with the partner rather than an immediate cut-off. Why it matters: this is the most directly relevant story in months, not an analogy — a real example of a supplier's own commission marketplace build, on the exact platform Booker Marketplace runs on, triggering a channel-conflict threat from an existing distribution partner. Worth a direct look at whether any of Booker's own suppliers selling through other marketplaces could face, or pose, the same conflict as Booker Marketplace's own third-party seller base grows.

- **USPS confirmed a further round of shipping rate rises for January 2027, including a permanent fuel surcharge, a day after reporting a $6.5bn net loss for FY2026 (10–12 November).** USPS's FY2026 results (year ended 30 September, released 10 November) showed a net loss of $6.5bn, improved from $9bn the prior year, on operating revenue up 3% to $82.9bn and operating expenses down 0.4% to $89.4bn; Shipping and Packages revenue rose 5.1% to a $1.7bn gain despite handling 359m fewer packages (down 5.3%), as average revenue per package rose about 11% to $5.28. Two days later (12 November), USPS filed new rates with the Postal Regulatory Commission (Docket CP2027-1) taking effect 17 January 2027: domestic Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, Ground Advantage and Parcel Select prices rise an average of 5.9%, and international parcel prices rise an average of 7.9%. The existing 8% transportation-related surcharge introduced in April becomes a permanent fuel surcharge, rising to 11%, with a review set for July 2027 based on first-half fuel prices; the surcharge does not apply to international products. Why it matters: a baseline carrier-cost rise landing a week into the holiday trading quarter, on top of (not instead of) the flat percentage increases most marketplaces already pass through to sellers — worth factoring into any shipping-cost assumptions for Booker Marketplace's own suppliers heading into the new year, given how directly USPS pricing already feeds into eBay, Etsy and Poshmark seller economics tracked repeatedly in this digest.
