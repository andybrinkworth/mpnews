*Covering 19–22 September 2026, since the last digest (plus one 18 September story — the EU/Amazon pricing probe — that broke too late to make that entry).*

### Top 3

- **EU Commission scrutinises Amazon over suspected seller price-parity practices under the Digital Markets Act (first reported 18 Sept, continued coverage through 21 Sept).** Brussels is examining whether Amazon algorithmically suppresses the visibility of listings from third-party sellers who price lower on their own site or on competing platforms — the kind of "effective price parity" the DMA explicitly bans for gatekeepers. It's preliminary: no formal investigation or finding of wrongdoing yet. *Why it matters: this is a different DMA angle from the Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper ruling already in the background section — here the target is Amazon's own ranking/visibility mechanics, which is closer to home for any marketplace that ranks sellers by price competitiveness.*

- **Amazon took a stake in Rappi, the Colombian delivery and quick-commerce platform (reported 19 Sept).** The investment gives Amazon a foothold in Latin American last-mile delivery and local marketplace infrastructure rather than building it from scratch. *Why it matters: another example of a dominant marketplace buying into local delivery/marketplace infrastructure instead of building it — relevant to how Booker Marketplace might think about build-vs-partner for fulfilment reach.*

- **Costco's delivery partnerships with Uber Eats and DoorDash went nationwide across the US (reported from 17 Sept, continuing into this window).** Both apps now offer same-day delivery from Costco warehouses across the country, not just pilot markets. *Why it matters: another big-box retailer routing fulfilment through third-party gig platforms rather than its own last-mile network — a model worth watching if Booker ever considers gig-delivery partners for its own supplier base.*

### Wider roundup

- J.C. Penney's Q2 earnings (reported 21 Sept) revealed its new third-party marketplace — soft-launched in late June with 100+ sellers in clothing, footwear and accessories — is "outperforming expectations" even as overall Q2 net sales fell over 8%. Beauty, electronics, home and outdoor categories are due to follow into 2027. Another data point for the "struggling retailer bolts on a marketplace" pattern already flagged with B&Q and Sur La Table in the background section.

- Amazon Accelerate 2026, Amazon's annual seller conference, opened in Seattle today (22 Sept) and runs through 24 Sept. Speakers are expected to cover Seller Central UI updates, an expanded AI Seller Assistant, and new data tools, but no concrete announcements had landed as of this digest — worth checking the next entry.

- Kheops, a French retail-tech platform connecting independent stores directly with suppliers outside centralised purchasing groups, raised a €15m (~$17m) round (21 Sept) from Odyssée Venture, ISAI and Elaia. It says order volume on its platform has quadrupled over the past year (revenue tripled) across 500+ stores and 3,000+ suppliers, and plans to use that data to recommend store-level assortments and anticipate ordering demand.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the FTC v Amazon ad-pricing suit, the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, or Shein/Temu litigation — those remain as recorded in the background section.

### B2B relevance for Booker Marketplace

- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this week — nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 19–22 September window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- Kheops is the closest thing to a direct analogue this week: a platform whose whole pitch is connecting stores and suppliers outside traditional centralised purchasing, using the resulting order data to recommend assortments and anticipate demand. That data-driven assortment/demand angle is worth watching as a feature direction for Booker Marketplace, since it sits squarely in the "independent retailer plus supplier network" space Booker already occupies.

- The Amazon–Rappi and Costco–Uber/DoorDash moves both point the same way: large platforms are increasingly buying or partnering into last-mile/delivery infrastructure rather than building it themselves. Worth keeping in mind for any conversation about how Booker Marketplace's own fulfilment model evolves.

- The EU's DMA scrutiny of Amazon's price-ranking mechanics is a reminder that how a marketplace ranks or surfaces sellers by price is now a live regulatory question in Europe, not just a UX/algorithm choice — relevant background if Booker Marketplace's own ranking or search logic ever weights price competitiveness heavily.
