From 1 November, anyone shipping into the EU from outside it must supply a Merchant Product Identifier, a standardised code (GTIN/EAN/UPC/ISBN) where one exists, and otherwise a manufacturer-assigned Non-Standardised Product Identifier - regardless of parcel value, and across all customs declaration types, not just low-value ones. eBay says it will supply the Merchant Product Identifier itself but has not said what sellers should enter when no manufacturer-level identifier exists at all, which is common for older clothing, vintage collectibles, worn vehicle parts and handmade goods.

Why it matters: this is a second, distinct EU compliance layer landing on the same date as the already-confirmed €2 Union Handling Fee - and unlike a per-item fee, missing product-identifier data can delay or block customs clearance outright, a sharper risk for any recommerce or long-tail marketplace than a cost increase would be.
