*Covering 20–23 October 2026, since the last digest. This window was less thin than several recent ones — a genuine agentic-commerce news cluster broke on 21 October — though UK wholesale/B2B competitor news stayed thin again and nothing is forced in below.*

**Top 3**

  - **Walmart opens its marketplace to AI shopping agents with a new Agent Gateway and Agent Profiles, explicitly contrasting its approach with Amazon's (21 Oct).** CEO John Furner announced the Agent Gateway — an MCP server, built on the protocol Anthropic originally created — at the Masters of Scale Summit in San Francisco, covering both Walmart and Sam's Club. Approved AI agents can search Walmart's inventory and buy on a shopper's behalf without the agent seeing the customer's personal information or purchase history directly. Agent Profiles, rolling out next month, give each agent its own record inside a customer's account so its purchases appear separately from the shopper's own; customers can require approval on every purchase or pre-authorise spending up to a limit they set (Walmart's own examples cite figures such as $50, $100 or $200, not fixed tiers), and can delete an agent's profile at any time. Agents cannot access payment-card data, must act within the shopper's stated intent, and will be blocked if they don't comply; Walmart's normal returns process still applies even when an agent buys the wrong thing. No Walmart+ membership is required. The Gateway is currently an invitation-only beta, alongside Walmart's existing integrations with Meta's Muse, ChatGPT and Gemini. Amazon, by contrast, has been blocking outside shopping agents (it cut off Meta's Muse in September, citing undisclosed access and credential capture) rather than building a sanctioned path for them. *Why it matters: this is the most concrete, productised answer yet to "how should an AI agent buy from a marketplace" — a named consent model (per-agent profiles, spend caps, approval gates, revocable access) rather than an outright block. For any commission-model marketplace, it's a template worth studying directly: who signs off when the buyer is an agent, and how commission and liability attach when something goes wrong.*

  - **Anthropic launches Claude Duet, a proactive AI agent app, the same day as Walmart's announcement (21 Oct).** Claude Duet is a US-only research preview, on a Pro/Max-subscriber waitlist, in which Claude works in the background rather than waiting to be opened: it sends a morning brief, checks in every few hours, and only messages when something needs attention. It runs on its own cloud virtual machine (so a phone or laptop doesn't need to stay on), carries over a user's existing memory, skills and connected apps — Anthropic cites more than 6,000 connectors, including Gmail, Slack, QuickBooks and Shopify — and shows the user the planned action and waits for approval before sending messages, making payments, signing documents or deleting anything. It joins a crowded field of "always-on" agent apps launched by rivals this year (Meta's Muse, Grok Bot, OpenAI's Dots). *Why it matters: this isn't a shopping product, but its Shopify and QuickBooks connectors and its pre-payment approval step show the same "agent acts, human approves" pattern Walmart just built for shopping specifically — further evidence that approval-gated agent autonomy, not full autonomy, is becoming the industry's working default for anything touching money.*

  - **Meta's revised commercial terms still permit broad website scraping that could feed AI training, legal experts say, despite Meta softening the wording — and the terms take effect 30 October with no advertiser opt-out (reported 15–20 Oct).** After Nieman Lab's 15 October report that Meta's upcoming terms for business/advertiser accounts could permit using scraped content to "train" its products, Meta published a revision on 16 October dropping the word "train" and narrowing the stated scope to its advertising and business products. Legal scholars quoted since then (Matt Topic, James Grimmelmann, Benjamin Sobel) say the change doesn't meaningfully narrow Meta's rights: "develop" is broad enough to cover training, "business product" is undefined, and the "associated with your products or services" language could still cover an entire website. Meta says robots.txt settings are unaffected and still let publishers block its crawler, but compliance with robots.txt is voluntary, not legally enforceable. Publisher groups (the News/Media Alliance, the UK News Media Association, the European Publishers Council) are pushing back, calling the terms an abuse of market power; businesses cannot opt out before the 30 October effective date without stopping advertising on Meta altogether. *Why it matters: this is the second digest running to carry a story about a major platform formalising, in terms a user can't negotiate, how it may use business/advertiser data for AI — after eBay's AI-training disclosure update in the last entry. The pattern (quiet or contested policy language, no real opt-out, scrutiny after the fact) is exactly what any marketplace should want to avoid when it eventually writes its own position on supplier/buyer data and AI.*

**B2B relevance for Booker Marketplace**

  - Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window — nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 20–23 October window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

  - Walmart's Agent Gateway is the one to study directly, not just note: it's a working, named model for consented agentic buying on a commission-model retailer's own platform — per-agent profiles, customer-set spend limits, mandatory approval gates, revocable access, and clear liability terms (the normal returns process still applies if an agent buys wrongly). Booker Marketplace will face the same questions eventually — who authorises an agent to buy on a wholesaler's behalf, how commission is attributed, what happens when an agent gets it wrong — and Walmart has just shipped a concrete reference design rather than a concept.

  - Claude Duet's approval-before-payment pattern, and its direct Shopify/QuickBooks connectors, are a reminder that "agent acts, human approves" is becoming a default well beyond shopping specifically — including the back-office and procurement tools adjacent to how Booker's own buyers operate.

  - Meta's contested AI-training terms are a second, reinforcing data point (after eBay's AI-training disclosure update) that platforms are moving ahead of their users on how buyer/seller/advertiser data feeds AI products, and getting challenged for it after the fact rather than before. Worth continuing to treat as a governance item to get ahead of, not react to.

  - The upcoming earnings window (eBay and Etsy, 28 Oct; Amazon, 29 Oct) and Amazon Business's Reshape conference (27–28 Oct) remain the most likely near-term sources of substantial new marketplace data and B2B announcements — flagged again for the next digest.
