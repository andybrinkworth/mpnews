Texas Comptroller Don Huffines signed an executive order on 30 September directing his office to amend Rule 3.330, which since October 2025 has classified certain marketplace seller fees - the commission Amazon, eBay, Etsy, Poshmark and others charge for listing, offering or facilitating a sale - as taxable "data processing" services. The proposed amendment, due for publication in the Texas Register on 16 October with a public-comment deadline of 15 November, would state plainly that marketplace fees are not data processing or information services. Huffines calls the existing tax a hidden, voter-unapproved burden on small sellers; his predecessor had defended it as applying existing law to two separate taxable transactions (the retail sale, and the seller's service). Sellers must keep paying the tax under the current rule until the process concludes, and it's unclear whether any refund would be retroactive.

Why it matters: this is a regulator moving in the opposite direction from most of what this digest tracks - reclassifying a marketplace's take-rate as a commission rather than a taxable service, rather than adding a new cost. It's a reminder that how a jurisdiction defines "the marketplace fee" for tax purposes is still unsettled and contestable, not a fixed category - worth knowing given how much of this digest's running thread (France's levy, the EU's Union Handling Fee, Colombia's VAT fight) has gone the other way.
