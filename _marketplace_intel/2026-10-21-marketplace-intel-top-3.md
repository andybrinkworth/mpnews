- **DoorDash reinvents itself as a general retail marketplace, adding Macy's, Anthropologie and VF Corp brands plus a paid returns service (1 Oct).** At its "Dash Forward" event in San Francisco, DoorDash said Macy's (350+ stores), Anthropologie (220+ stores) and VF Corporation brands including The North Face, Vans and Timberland (350+ stores) are now live on its marketplace, alongside a new partnership with Costco that brings eight of the top ten US grocers onto the platform. It also launched "Dasher Returns": a courier can collect an eligible return in as little as 30 minutes for a flat $7.99 fee, carrying it back to the originating store for restocking and a refund within days; the service is live in select cities now, with nationwide expansion due in November. DoorDash said over half of the National Retail Federation's Top 100 Retailers now sell through it, and roughly 90% of a retailer's first-time DoorDash shoppers are incremental customers. Why it matters: a delivery platform is explicitly repositioning its existing driver and store network as general-purpose retail and reverse-logistics infrastructure — its chief revenue officer described it as turning "the stores they already have into a growth channel, not a warehouse three states away" — a live example of marketplace-as-a-service and outsourced returns handling arriving in the same announcement.

- **DoorDash opens an agentic-ordering connector built specifically for corporate procurement (1 Oct).** At the same event, DoorDash launched a corporate version of its Model Context Protocol (MCP) connector, letting companies wire automated ordering — food, office supplies — directly into tools like Slack. Co-founder Andy Fang said it lets firms "bring agentic ordering into tools their teams are already working in." Five companies — SpaceX, Cognition, Vercel, Mercor and Tempo — are testing it; a public waitlist opened 30 September. Individual employees must still sign into their own DoorDash account and accept terms of service before an agent can order on their behalf. Why it matters: the most directly transferable agentic-commerce story this digest has tracked — a named, live integration aimed squarely at B2B/corporate buying rather than consumer shopping, with an explicit identity and consent model (per-employee sign-in and sign-off) that is a plausible template for how a wholesale marketplace might eventually let a buyer's own ordering system or AI agent place orders.

- **Temu's UK accounts show revenue up 171% as the government confirms the duty exemption it has relied on will close — just not until 2028 (5 Oct).** Temu's first full UK accounts show revenue of $171m for 2025 (up from $63m in 2024, reflecting transaction and marketing fees from its merchants rather than total consumer spend), profit up from $3m to $6m, and UK headcount growing from zero to 31 employees. Separately, Barclays/Retail Economics research estimates UK shoppers spend £4.7bn a year across Temu, Shein and similar ultra-low-cost marketplaces — about 5% of non-food e-commerce. The UK government confirmed in July 2026 that it will scrap the £135 customs-duty exemption on low-value imports and bring in new low-value import rules by 2028; parcels using that exemption were worth £3bn in 2024/25, up from £1.3bn the year before. Why it matters: concrete evidence of how large the low-cost marketplace channel has become in the UK specifically, and confirmation that the UK is following the US, France, Colombia and the EU in closing the de-minimis loophole — but on a markedly slower timeline (2028) than everyone else already in this digest's running thread.
