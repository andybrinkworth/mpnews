- **eBay rolls out a new FCC equipment-ID requirement that can hide non-compliant electronics listings from US buyers, effective 13 October (reported 8 Oct).** Listings for devices that intentionally transmit radio signals (phones, laptops, tablets, routers, drones, Bluetooth devices, wireless cameras, smartwatches, two-way radios, remote-controlled toys) must show a valid FCC ID at the point of sale; sellers who can't confirm one may be unable to create, revise or relist, and non-compliant listings "may be hidden from buyers in the US." This follows an FCC Third Report and Order (first reported in July) that, for the first time, puts compliance obligations directly on marketplaces as well as sellers: a marketplace with physical access to the goods or that takes title is itself liable for displaying a valid, accurate FCC ID, while a marketplace with no physical access or title must display an ID but isn't liable for a seller's inaccuracies if it does reasonable due diligence. Why it matters: this is a regulator formally splitting compliance liability between platform and seller based on how much control the platform has over the goods — the first concrete rule of its kind tracked in this digest, and a template other product-safety regulators (in the US or elsewhere) could copy for other marketplaces.

- **eBay's 2026 Recommerce Report finds secondhand shopping has gone mainstream, not niche (7 Oct).** Surveying over 10,500 consumers across 10 countries, eBay found 76% bought secondhand in the past year, with 47% buying at least monthly — up from 35% in 2025; 70% see secondhand shopping as important for managing household budgets (36% of US respondents call it "very important"); and 89% plan to spend the same or more on secondhand next year, rising to 53% of 18–29-year-olds expecting to buy more. On the seller side, 45% of surveyed US eBay sellers say resale provides supplemental income and 25% say it's vital household income. Why it matters: hard survey data, not anecdote, behind a trend this digest has tracked all year (Vinted/Depop's shipping price war, Sur La Table's resale launch, J.C. Penney's marketplace) — recommerce is now a budget-management habit for a large share of shoppers, not a sustainability niche.

- **eBay published a run of smaller seller-programme updates across three markets within a couple of days (6 Oct).** In the US, qualifying auto-parts recyclers can now earn "Certified Recycled" status, giving them special badging, a dedicated search filter and a search-ranking boost; a new "Used – Part on Vehicle" condition was also added for parts still fitted to a donor vehicle. In the UK, the Authenticity Guarantee programme for trading cards now covers cards valued at £250 and above, down from the previous £500 threshold, alongside wider rollout of Bundle Discounts. In Germany, sellers can now generate up to 200 shipping labels at once in Seller Hub Pro, an updated returns policy for vehicle parts has applied since 28 September, and a dedicated vehicle-parts "Fitment Service" is due to start in mid-November. Why it matters: none of these is individually significant, but together they show a marketplace competing for seller loyalty within specific categories — certification badges, lower authentication thresholds, bulk tooling — rather than only through broad fee moves, which is a cheaper lever to pull than a take-rate cut.
