**Top 3**

- **Mirakl appointed Steve Stathis Tzikakis as co-CEO alongside founder Philippe Corrot, with co-founder Adrien Nussenbaum stepping back to Chairman of the Board — announced 10 November 2026, and missed by this digest's four most recent prior entries.** Under the new structure, Philippe Corrot remains co-founder and CEO, keeping Strategy, Product, Tech and Customer Success, and continuing to oversee Mirakl Connect and Mirakl Ads; Tzikakis, reporting to Corrot, takes on Marketing & Sales, Finance, Alliances & Partners and Human Resources — the scope Nussenbaum previously held before transitioning out of his executive responsibilities earlier in the year. Nussenbaum becomes Chairman (the release's own quote attribution separately calls him "Executive Chairman," an inconsistency in Mirakl's own wording), remains a significant shareholder, and says he will now spend more time on philanthropy and film production. Tzikakis was previously CEO of Sitecore, where Mirakl's release says he doubled ARR to €500m; before that he spent 13 years at SAP, including as President of EMEA South, where he is credited with doubling a €4bn regional business across 75 countries. Mirakl frames the move around its positioning as "the operating system for intelligent commerce," with Tzikakis quoted saying "as the industry enters the agentic era, no company is better positioned to redefine what's possible, and for Mirakl that time is now," and Corrot describing the split as combining "product and technology vision with Steve's commercial and operational leadership" ahead of the company's "next phase of growth." No updated Mirakl financials (ARR, GMV) were given in the release itself. *Why it matters: this is the platform Booker Marketplace itself runs on, not an analogy — a change at the top of a vendor this central is worth a direct conversation with the Mirakl account team about roadmap continuity, support, and the Mirakl Nexus agentic-commerce push already flagged in this digest's 16 October entry, rather than being picked up third-hand a fortnight after the fact, as happened here.*

- **eBay's rewritten global privacy policy took effect yesterday, 26 November 2026, expanding AI-training and automated-monitoring language while quietly dropping its standing promise to notify users directly of material changes.** eBay emailed the changes to users on 19 October, with UK, Australia and Canada versions taking effect on the same date. Automated content monitoring — previously framed around messages — now explicitly covers "listings, images, videos, livestreams, comments, reactions, transcripts, captions, reports, and other user-generated content," paired with spelled-out enforcement powers (blocking messages, restricting account functions) and a new line reserving the right to "limit the level of detail" eBay provides about its own detection and enforcement systems. On AI, eBay says it "relies primarily on publicly available information" for generative AI training but may also use certain de-identified user personal data — listed, sold, viewed or bought items — and has dropped both its earlier explicit reference to training third-party AI models and its promise to tell users when they're interacting with an AI. Most notably, the clause committing to notify registered users of material changes "via My Messages in My eBay and/or by email" has been removed; the policy now only says eBay "may" post a revised notice with an effective date. An opt-out exists at eBay's AI-preferences page, but it is framed for EEA/UK/Switzerland users, its applicability to US sellers is unclear, and it doesn't reverse training already completed or stop processing for fraud, safety or legal purposes. *Why it matters: this is the fourth or fifth marketplace this digest has tracked (after Etsy, Depop and Meta, and eBay's own October disclosure update) expanding AI-data rights while narrowing its own notice obligations — reinforcing the governance lesson this digest keeps returning to: get Booker's own supplier/buyer AI-data policy right, with clear scope and real notice commitments, before publishing it, not after sellers notice what's missing.*

- **eBay and Whatnot are running head-to-head 0% commission promotions over the Black Friday weekend (27–30 November), each shaped differently.** eBay waives commission and its fixed per-order fee on eBay Live sales completed on eBay.com (US) on Black Friday itself, Pacific Time — a single day — though its 2.9% payment processing fee still applies. Whatnot runs a four-day structure: 0% commission for all sellers on Black Friday; 0% for standard-tier sellers only (excluding those on volume discounts or custom rates) on "Small Seller Saturday," 28 November; and 50% off standard commission on 29–30 November; its $0.30 per-transaction fee applies throughout. Whatnot is separately putting up to $1m in gift cards into the hands of roughly 10,000 small US/Canada sellers to give away during their own livestreams, aimed at helping smaller sellers draw viewers away from bigger ones. For context, Whatnot reported over $100m in Live sales in the 48 hours around Black Friday 2025, up 185% year-on-year; eBay does not disclose quarterly Live GMV and has not announced a comparable giveaway this year. *Why it matters: both platforms are cutting take-rate specifically to win sellers' best inventory during the single highest-value selling weekend of the year — a reminder that commission is a lever marketplaces pull hardest exactly when sellers have the most leverage, worth weighing when designing the timing of any promotional commission discount on Booker Marketplace.*

**B2B relevance for Booker Marketplace**

- The Mirakl leadership change is the one to raise internally rather than just note: a core infrastructure vendor splitting its top team between a product/tech-focused CEO and a commercial/operational co-CEO is worth a direct conversation about roadmap continuity and support, rather than being left to surface through press coverage a fortnight late, as happened here.

- eBay's privacy policy rewrite is another entry in a now-familiar list — Etsy, Depop, Meta, and eBay itself twice — of marketplaces expanding AI-data rights while narrowing notice obligations to users. Worth continuing to treat Booker's own eventual supplier/buyer AI-data policy as something to get right before publishing, with real scope and real notice commitments, rather than reacting to a seller backlash afterwards.

- The eBay/Whatnot Black Friday commission war is a transferable, time-bound idea: cutting take-rate specifically around the highest-value trading moments of the year is a direct, visible way to win seller loyalty and inventory when competition for it is fiercest — worth considering whether a similarly time-boxed commission discount around Booker's own peak trading periods could help retain supplier volume against competitor pulls.
