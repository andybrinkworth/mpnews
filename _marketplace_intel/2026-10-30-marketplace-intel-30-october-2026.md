**Top 3**

- **Amazon's Q3 2026 results show AWS reaccelerating sharply and operating margin hitting a record, while AI capital spending balloons and advertising growth slows (29 Oct).** Net sales were $201.9bn, up 12%; operating income was $28.1bn, giving a record 13.9% operating margin. AWS revenue jumped 39% to $46.0bn — its fastest growth since 2021 — with AWS operating income of $17.8bn (38.7% margin) now accounting for roughly 63% of Amazon's total operating profit from about 23% of sales; CEO Andy Jassy guided to "over 40%" AWS growth in both 2027 and 2028. Advertising revenue grew 11% to $19.6bn, down sharply from 26% growth the prior quarter — Amazon attributes the slowdown mainly to Prime Day having shifted into Q2 this year. Capital expenditure hit $77.3bn in the quarter (versus $35.1bn a year earlier), with full-year capex now guided to roughly $220bn; trailing-12-month free cash flow was negative $42.4bn versus positive $14.8bn a year ago, and long-term debt has nearly tripled since the end of 2025 to $173.1bn. *Why it matters: Amazon's core retail/marketplace business is now financially a side effect of its AI infrastructure story — margin, cash flow and debt are all being reshaped by AWS capex rather than by marketplace mechanics. The advertising slowdown is also worth watching: ad revenue has been one of the fastest-growing, highest-margin lines for every big marketplace, Amazon included, and a Prime-Day-timing explanation is plausible but worth checking again next quarter.*

- **Amazon Business used its Reshape 2026 conference to push further into "prove the value, not just the price" procurement tooling (27–28 Oct, Nashville).** Two launches: an expanded Amazon Business Assistant that now works directly with a customer's own order history, spend and approvals — adding order tracking, approval-process visibility, savings recommendations and payment-allocation options; and a new "Amazon Business Value Intelligence" reporting tool that combines a buyer organisation's own purchasing data with Amazon's third-party benchmarks to quantify savings, operational efficiency and purchase compliance, aimed at helping procurement teams justify the platform to finance and management. Both are live for US Amazon Business customers through their account pages. *Why it matters: this is Amazon Business explicitly building the back-office case for why a company should keep buying through its marketplace — benchmarked savings reporting and compliance tooling, not just catalogue breadth or price — which is precisely the kind of buyer-retention lever a commission-model B2B marketplace competes on.*

- **eBay and Etsy both reported solid Q3 2026 marketplace growth on 28 October, with Etsy's active buyer base returning to growth for the first time in two years.** eBay: revenue $3.21bn (+14% reported, +13% FX-neutral), GMV $23.2bn (+15% reported, +16% FX-neutral), first-party ad revenue $575m (+16%), and active buyers of 147m (+10%, or +1% excluding the newly-consolidated Depop and Tise); eBay raised full-year guidance to $12.62bn–$12.67bn revenue and $91.5bn–$91.9bn GMV, and said eBay Live is now running at a multibillion-dollar annualised GMV rate with triple-digit growth. Etsy: revenue $692.5m (+10.9%), marketplace GMS of $2.65bn (+8.8% reported, +9.1% FX-neutral), take rate up 50 basis points to 26.2%, and trailing-12-month active buyers up 1.0% to roughly 88m — the first year-over-year buyer increase in two years, which CEO Kruti Patel Goyal credited partly to personalisation (an estimated +2 percentage points of GMS growth) and to search-relevance and quality-signal improvements. Etsy's reported net income of $853.5m was inflated by the one-off $839.7m pre-tax gain on its Depop sale to eBay, which closed 30 July; continuing-operations net income was $113.6m. *Why it matters: after a stretch where this digest has repeatedly flagged eBay cutting staff while growing and Etsy restructuring around a shrunken core, both companies now have genuine, non-one-off evidence — Etsy's buyer growth, eBay's GMV growth and raised guidance — that the post-Depop-split strategies (eBay as the "everything" marketplace absorbing resale and live-selling, Etsy as a leaner specialist marketplace betting on personalisation) are working, at least for one quarter.*

**Wider roundup**

- Meta delayed the effective date of its contested AI-training/website-crawling commercial terms from 30 October to 20 November (announced 23 October) — resolving, for now, the running ambiguity this digest flagged in the last two entries. Meta says the extra time is to explain the changes to partners and clarify how its crawlers and robots.txt controls work; it has not said it will change the underlying text, and UK publisher groups called the delay "a pause, not a solution." The European Commission welcomed the delay.

- TikTok Shop Malaysia's commission-rate changes for most categories took effect from 31 October (rates reported 19 October): increases on discretionary categories such as fragrance, make-up and men's/nail/foot care (18.90% → 20.52%) and women's tops and dresses for marketplace sellers (10.26% → 12.74%); cuts on feminine care (14.58% → 12.10%) and nursing & feeding (9.72% → 6.80%); and pantry food, baking, fresh/frozen food, dairy and cooking staples staying at 0%. A new Beer, Wine & Spirits category launches at 9.51%.

- Amazon is reassigning some ASINs to different fee categories from 31 October, which can raise or lower referral fees without any change to the listing itself; Amazon is notifying affected sellers directly and recommends checking the Fee Preview report in Seller Central ahead of peak trading.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the FTC's separate Amazon monopolisation case (still on its 29 March 2027 trial date), the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, the EU Commission's Amazon price-parity probe, France's fast-fashion levy, the EU's confirmed 1 November Union Handling Fee (€2 per item) or the EU's mandatory customs Product Identifier rule (also 1 November) — those all remain as previously recorded. Texas's proposed repeal of its marketplace-fee tax (Rule 3.330) also remains in its public-comment period (through 15 November).

- Shopify reports Q3 2026 results on 3 November and Walmart on 19 November — both flagged for the next digest.

**B2B relevance for Booker Marketplace**

- Amazon Business's two Reshape launches are the most directly transferable items this week: the Value Intelligence benchmarking tool in particular is a concrete answer to "how do we prove this marketplace is worth it beyond price," which is exactly the pitch a commission-model B2B platform needs for buyers whose finance teams want evidence, not just a lower unit cost.

- Amazon's AWS-driven capex surge (to roughly $220bn for the year, with free cash flow now deeply negative) and its slowing ad-revenue growth are a reminder that even the largest marketplace's financial story is now being written by AI infrastructure spending rather than retail/marketplace fundamentals — worth bearing in mind when benchmarking Booker's own commission economics against "what Amazon does," since Amazon's own incentives are increasingly pointed elsewhere.

- Etsy's return to buyer growth, credited to personalisation and search-relevance work rather than a pricing or fee change, is a useful proof point for Booker: better discovery and search, not just commission structure, can directly move active-buyer numbers.

- TikTok Shop Malaysia's category-by-category commission changes — raising rates on discretionary goods while cutting them on essentials and holding staples at zero — is a transferable commission-design pattern: differentiating take-rate by category to protect low-margin essentials while capturing more from discretionary spend is a lever worth considering as Booker's own commission schedule matures.
