- **Amazon Business adds a nationally-solicited county-government cooperative purchasing contract, extending its reach into public-sector procurement (19 Oct).** Via Public Promise Procurement, a National Association of Counties (NACo) programme, Amazon Business now offers US county governments a member-governed, peer-reviewed cooperative contract giving access to 300m+ products and hundreds of thousands of third-party sellers, with fixed-term price ceilings across eight product categories (not named in available reporting) and tools letting agencies prioritise local and in-state suppliers. Douglas County, Nebraska led the nationally-solicited contract; counties activate it through their existing Amazon Business account team, alongside Prime membership. Why it matters: this is Amazon wrapping its commission marketplace in a publicly-procured, framework-style contract — complete with price ceilings and local-supplier preference tooling — rather than just competing on price and delivery. That combination (a cooperative/framework wrapper around an open marketplace) is exactly the kind of structure a commission-model B2B marketplace chasing institutional or procurement-governed buyers would want to study.

- **USPS Ground Advantage replaces its rural/standard zip classifications with three new geographic pricing groups from 18 October, moving marketplace sellers' shipping costs with it (change reported from 15 Oct, still being tested by sellers as of 17 Oct).** The new Geo B, C and D groups together cover 25,491 US zip codes; Geo C and D match the prior rural list exactly, while Geo B adds 7,625 zip codes that previously had standard pricing. eBay, whose sellers are most affected, says the change isn't an overall rate rise — some destinations get cheaper, some more expensive — but as of the most recent reporting it hadn't published rate cards or worked examples, and sellers offering free or flat-rate shipping have no clear way yet to tell whether they need to adjust listing prices to cover the new rates. Why it matters: a reminder that underlying carrier rate structures, not just marketplace policy, can quietly move seller/supplier shipping economics by postcode or zone — landing right ahead of peak trading, when shipping-cost assumptions matter most.

- **eBay widened its global privacy policy to expand disclosures on AI training, automated content monitoring and enforcement transparency (19 Oct).** Detail in available reporting is limited — the fuller write-up sits behind a paywall — but the headline change is that eBay now discloses more about using buyer/seller data to train AI models, alongside expanded automated-monitoring and enforcement-transparency language. Why it matters: another large marketplace formalising, in its own terms, how buyer/seller data may be used to train AI — a policy question every marketplace will increasingly need its own clear, visible answer to, not just Amazon and Meta.
