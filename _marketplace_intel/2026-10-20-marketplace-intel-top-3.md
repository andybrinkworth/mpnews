- **Amazon Accelerate 2026: Seller Central opens to Walmart, eBay, Shopify and TikTok (24 Sept).** Independent sellers can now connect their Walmart, eBay, Shopify and TikTok Shop accounts to Seller Central, import and link listings, see orders from every channel in one place, and fulfil across platforms — free, rolling out to US sellers over the coming months. Syncing listings across channels, AI-generated cross-platform listings and a consolidated per-channel earnings view are planned next. Why it matters: Amazon is repositioning Seller Central as the operating system for a seller's whole business, not just their Amazon storefront — useful competitive intelligence on how a dominant marketplace tries to make itself indispensable even to sellers who trade elsewhere too.

- **Amazon blocked Meta's Muse AI shopping agent (21–22 Sept), then opened its own seller tools to Anthropic's Claude (23 Sept).** Amazon cut off Meta's Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.com over the weekend, saying Meta never disclosed Muse would access the site, that the agent doesn't identify itself when browsing, and that it captures customer credentials without clear consent. Two days later, at Accelerate, Amazon announced the opposite move for a partner it controls: a new plugin lets sellers manage their Amazon business through Anthropic's Claude or Amazon's own Quick assistant, with sellers choosing what data the agent can see and approving every action. Why it matters: Amazon is setting the rules for agentic commerce on its own marketplace — which AI agents may transact, on what terms, with what disclosure and consent. It has done the same to Perplexity, Google and OpenAI over the past year. Any marketplace, B2B included, will eventually need its own answer to "which AI agents can buy or sell on us, and how."

- **Instacart pushes retailers to cut online markups to grow grocery delivery adoption (reported 25 Sept).** CEO Chris Rogers said retailer price markups online are "one of the biggest challenges" to customer adoption; Instacart's own data shows retailers who don't mark up items online grow roughly 10 percentage points faster and retain customers longer. Instacart is working with retail partners to bring markups down, which retailers can then reinvest in loyalty and marketing, alongside its own affordability moves (delivery from $10 via Instacart Plus, in-store pickup, no-rush delivery). Why it matters: a live data point on the trade-off between marketplace/platform margin and conversion — relevant to any commission-model platform weighing take-rate against price transparency for buyers.
