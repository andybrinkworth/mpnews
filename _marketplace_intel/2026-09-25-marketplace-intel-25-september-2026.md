(Covering 23-25 September 2026, since the last digest)

### TOP 3 HEADLINES

1\. Amazon Accelerate 2026: Seller Central opens to Walmart, eBay, Shopify and TikTok (24 Sept)

Amazon's annual seller conference produced its main announcement: independent sellers can now connect their Walmart, eBay, Shopify and TikTok Shop accounts to Seller Central, import and link listings, see orders from every channel in one place, and fulfil across platforms - free, rolling out to US sellers over the coming months. Syncing listings across channels, AI-generated cross-platform listings and a consolidated per-channel earnings view are planned next.

Why it matters: Amazon is repositioning Seller Central as the operating system for a seller's whole business, not just their Amazon storefront - useful competitive intelligence on how a dominant marketplace tries to make itself indispensable even to sellers who trade elsewhere too.

2\. Amazon blocked Meta's Muse AI shopping agent (21-22 Sept), then opened its own seller tools to Anthropic's Claude (23 Sept)

Amazon cut off Meta's Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.com over the weekend, saying Meta never disclosed Muse would access the site, that the agent doesn't identify itself when browsing, and that it captures customer credentials without clear consent. Two days later, at Accelerate, Amazon announced the opposite move for a partner it controls: a new plugin lets sellers manage their Amazon business through Anthropic's Claude or Amazon's own Quick assistant, with sellers choosing what data the agent can see and approving every action.

Why it matters: Amazon is setting the rules for agentic commerce on its own marketplace - which AI agents may transact, on what terms, with what disclosure and consent. It has done the same to Perplexity, Google and OpenAI over the past year. Any marketplace, B2B included, will eventually need its own answer to "which AI agents can buy or sell on us, and how".

3\. Instacart pushes retailers to cut online markups to grow grocery delivery adoption (reported 25 Sept)

CEO Chris Rogers said retailer price markups online are "one of the biggest challenges" to customer adoption; Instacart's own data shows retailers who don't mark up items online grow roughly 10 percentage points faster and retain customers longer. Instacart is working with retail partners to bring markups down, which retailers can then reinvest in loyalty and marketing, alongside its own affordability moves (delivery from $10 via Instacart Plus, in-store pickup, no-rush delivery).

Why it matters: a live data point on the trade-off between marketplace/platform margin and conversion - relevant to any commission-model platform weighing take-rate against price transparency for buyers.

### WIDER ROUNDUP

- Etsy's September seller update (22 Sept) added Shared Shop Access (up to five collaborators per shop, with permission limits - no access to finances or refunds), a third listing variation and more customisable option fields, a redesigned payment/balance view, and wider eligibility for its listing-appeals process (aiming for most sellers to have access across most policy areas by end of 2026). Etsy also launched "Holiday HQ" (24 Sept), a hub to help sellers prepare for peak season.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the EU Commission's price-parity probe into Amazon (flagged 18-21 Sept, still preliminary), the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, or Shein/Temu litigation - those remain as previously recorded.

### RELEVANT TO A COMMISSION-MODEL B2B MARKETPLACE

- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window - nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 23-25 September window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- The Muse-block/Claude-open contrast is the most relevant story this week: Amazon is now explicitly gatekeeping which AI agents can transact on its marketplace, requiring disclosure, consent and per-action approval from approved partners. As buyer-side procurement agents become more common in B2B too, Booker Marketplace will eventually need its own policy on which agents can place orders on suppliers' behalf, what data they can see, and how commission is attributed when the "buyer" is an AI acting for a wholesaler.

- Amazon's multichannel Seller Central expansion is a reminder that platform stickiness increasingly comes from owning the seller's whole operational view, not just transaction volume on one channel. Worth asking whether a consolidated cross-channel view for Booker's suppliers (orders, listings, performance - even across non-Booker channels) could be a similar stickiness lever.

- Instacart's markup-reduction data is a useful external data point for any conversation about take-rate vs price transparency: retailers who kept prices undistorted online grew faster and retained customers better - relevant framing for how commission is presented to buyers on Booker Marketplace.
