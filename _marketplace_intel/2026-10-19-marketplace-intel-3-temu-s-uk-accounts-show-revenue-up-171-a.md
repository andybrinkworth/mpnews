Temu's first full UK accounts show revenue of $171m for 2025 (up from $63m in 2024, reflecting transaction and marketing fees from its merchants rather than total consumer spend), profit up from $3m to $6m, and UK headcount growing from zero to 31 employees. Separately, Barclays/Retail Economics research estimates UK shoppers spend £4.7bn a year across Temu, Shein and similar ultra-low-cost marketplaces - about 5% of non-food e-commerce. The UK government confirmed in July 2026 that it will scrap the £135 customs-duty exemption on low-value imports and bring in new low-value import rules by 2028; parcels using that exemption were worth £3bn in 2024/25, up from £1.3bn the year before.

Why it matters: concrete evidence of how large the low-cost marketplace channel has become in the UK specifically, and confirmation that the UK is following the US, France, Colombia and the EU in closing the de-minimis loophole - but on a markedly slower timeline (2028) than everyone else already in this digest's running thread.

### WIDER ROUNDUP

- The EU has now confirmed the exact size of the per-item "Union Handling Fee" flagged as still-to-be-set in the last digest: €2 per item, on top of the existing €3 duty, from 1 November 2026 (confirmed via an Irish Revenue notice, 29 Sept) - paid by whichever business files the customs declaration, regardless of the parcel's value.

- Shopify launched "Canvas" (1 Oct), which lets merchants build a storefront by chatting with its Sidekick AI rather than editing a theme directly, showing real-time rendered changes. It's desktop-only for now and doesn't yet support third-party themes, app blocks or extensions - it joins similar AI site-builders from Wix, Squarespace, Webflow and Framer.

- eBay has raised seller currency-conversion fees by 0.25 percentage points in every market it operates - US/Canada (3% to 3.25%, from 14 Oct), Australia (to 3.25%, 1 Nov), UK (2.5% to 2.75%, 1 Dec) and Germany/France/Italy/Spain (to 3.25%, 2 Dec) - without a formal Seller Update or Community post; the change was only added to each market's selling-fee page (reported 3 Oct).

- Amazon's UK Prime Big Deal Days ran 6-7 October, as flagged in the last digest; no performance figures are out yet.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, the EU Commission's Amazon price-parity probe, or France's fast-fashion levy - those remain as previously recorded. Separately, the long-running FTC monopoly/marketplace case against Amazon (distinct from the ad-auction suit) - once scheduled for an October 2026 trial - has since slipped to a proposed 29 March 2027, so nothing in that case is imminent despite older coverage still circulating with an October 2026 date.

### RELEVANT TO A COMMISSION-MODEL B2B MARKETPLACE

- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window - nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 30 September-6 October window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- DoorDash's corporate MCP connector is the standout item this week: a live, named example of agentic ordering built for procurement rather than consumer shopping, with a simple consent model - each employee signs into their own account and approves the connection before an agent can order for them. Worth watching as a possible shape for how Booker Marketplace eventually handles AI agents ordering on a wholesaler's behalf: who signs off, and how commission is attributed when the "buyer" is an agent.

- DoorDash's "Dasher Returns" is the second marketplace this month - after UPS-Happy Returns/TikTok Shop, covered in the last digest - to productise reverse logistics as a paid add-on rather than build it bespoke. Two separate, unrelated platforms reaching for the same solution in the same fortnight is worth noting if Booker's own returns/claims handling needs to scale.

- eBay's unannounced fee rise is a reminder that marketplaces sometimes change take-rate economics quietly rather than through a clear update - worth keeping in mind given Booker's commission model depends on supplier trust in how and when charges change.

- Temu's UK numbers and the confirmed-but-distant (2028) closure of the UK's own low-value import exemption are useful domestic context: the cheapest import channel is under the same long-term pressure here as everywhere else already tracked in this digest, just on a longer fuse.
