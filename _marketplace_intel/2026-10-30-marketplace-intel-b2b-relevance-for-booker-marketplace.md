- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window — nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 20–23 October window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- Walmart's Agent Gateway is the one to study directly, not just note: it's a working, named model for consented agentic buying on a commission-model retailer's own platform — per-agent profiles, customer-set spend limits, mandatory approval gates, revocable access, and clear liability terms (the normal returns process still applies if an agent buys wrongly). Booker Marketplace will face the same questions eventually — who authorises an agent to buy on a wholesaler's behalf, how commission is attributed, what happens when an agent gets it wrong — and Walmart has just shipped a concrete reference design rather than a concept.

- Claude Duet's approval-before-payment pattern, and its direct Shopify/QuickBooks connectors, are a reminder that "agent acts, human approves" is becoming a default well beyond shopping specifically — including the back-office and procurement tools adjacent to how Booker's own buyers operate.

- Meta's contested AI-training terms are a second, reinforcing data point (after eBay's AI-training disclosure update) that platforms are moving ahead of their users on how buyer/seller/advertiser data feeds AI products, and getting challenged for it after the fact rather than before. Worth continuing to treat as a governance item to get ahead of, not react to.

- The upcoming earnings window (eBay and Etsy, 28 Oct; Amazon, 29 Oct) and Amazon Business's Reshape conference (27–28 Oct) remain the most likely near-term sources of substantial new marketplace data and B2B announcements — flagged again for the next digest.
