- DoorDash's $17bn agreed acquisition of Instacart (announced 29 Oct, covered in the last digest) is "likely to draw an in-depth investigation from US federal and state antitrust regulators," according to MLex (30 Oct); the visible reporting cites risks of eliminating a growing competitor and raising delivery prices, but doesn't name specific agencies or give a review timeline — worth watching for a more concrete regulatory response in the coming weeks.

- Amazon quietly launched "Returnless Resolutions" for FBA sellers in the UK, Germany, Italy, France and Spain around 29 Oct, missed by the last two digests. It's opt-in per-ASIN or catalogue-wide: sellers set their own maximum item price, from €1 to €75, below which a buyer's refund request is granted without the item being sent back; kept-item refunds still count toward a seller's return rate but are exempted from Amazon's high-return-rate fee.

- Meta's contested Commercial Terms (the AI-training/website-crawling clause for advertisers) remain unresolved, with no material change since the 23–24 October delay of the effective date to 20 November; a 28 October report found Meta's UK/EU "object to processing" form in its privacy centre visible only via certain regions in testing, and some locale pages were still showing the original training wording as of mid-to-late October. No resolution is expected before 20 November.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the FTC's separate Amazon monopolisation case (still on its 29 March 2027 trial date), the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, the EU Commission's Amazon price-parity probe, or France's fast-fashion levy — those all remain as previously recorded. Texas's proposed repeal of its marketplace-fee tax (Rule 3.330) also remains in its public-comment period, through 15 November.

- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window — nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 4–6 November window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- Walmart reports Q3 2026 results on 19 November — flagged again for a future digest.
