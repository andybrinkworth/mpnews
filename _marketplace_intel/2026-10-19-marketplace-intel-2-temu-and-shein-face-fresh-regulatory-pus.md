Colombia's Chamber of E-Commerce is pressing the government to close what it calls unfair tax treatment of low-value parcels - a de-minimis exemption designed for occasional shipments that now underwrites an estimated 2.2 million Temu parcels a month. A temporary $50 VAT-exemption threshold was already struck down by Colombia's Constitutional Court in January 2026, reverting to a $200 limit for certain shipments, and industry groups are now pushing for platforms to collect VAT at checkout.

Why it matters: another country litigating the same de-minimis/duty-arbitrage question that reshaped US cross-border selling in 2025 and prompted France's fast-fashion levy in September - the low-cost cross-border marketplace model is being contested market by market, not settled once globally.
