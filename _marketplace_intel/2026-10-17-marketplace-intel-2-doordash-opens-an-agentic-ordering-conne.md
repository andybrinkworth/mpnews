At the same event, DoorDash launched a corporate version of its Model Context Protocol (MCP) connector, letting companies wire automated ordering - food, office supplies - directly into tools like Slack. Co-founder Andy Fang said it lets firms "bring agentic ordering into tools their teams are already working in." Five companies - SpaceX, Cognition, Vercel, Mercor and Tempo - are testing it; a public waitlist opened 30 September. Individual employees must still sign into their own DoorDash account and accept terms of service before an agent can order on their behalf.

Why it matters: the most directly transferable agentic-commerce story this digest has tracked - a named, live integration aimed squarely at B2B/corporate buying rather than consumer shopping, with an explicit identity and consent model (per-employee sign-in and sign-off) that is a plausible template for how a wholesale marketplace might eventually let a buyer's own ordering system or AI agent place orders.
