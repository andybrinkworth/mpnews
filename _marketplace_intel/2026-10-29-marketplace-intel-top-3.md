- **Adobe: US online holiday spending forecast to hit $275.1bn this season, up 6.7% year-on-year (28 Sept).** Adobe's holiday forecast (1 Nov–31 Dec) projects Cyber Week growing 7.4% to $47.5bn, with Cyber Monday alone passing $15bn online for the first time (+6.2%) and Black Friday up 9.2% to $12.9bn on electronics and appliances discounting. Adobe also flags a stockpiling trend layered on top of gift-buying: online sales of essentials are set to jump sharply during Cyber Week (personal care +150%, clothing basics +210%, baby products +113%, pet items +93%). Why it matters: a more bullish, more granular read than the eMarketer holiday forecast already on record (2.6% retail / 6.6% e-commerce growth) — the essentials-stockpiling pattern in particular suggests shoppers are treating online more like a general restocking channel, not just a gift channel, which is worth factoring into Q4 category and delivery-capacity planning.

- **Temu and Shein face fresh regulatory pushback in Colombia over VAT and de-minimis treatment (28 Sept).** Colombia's Chamber of E-Commerce is pressing the government to close what it calls unfair tax treatment of low-value parcels — a de-minimis exemption designed for occasional shipments that now underwrites an estimated 2.2 million Temu parcels a month. A temporary $50 VAT-exemption threshold was already struck down by Colombia's Constitutional Court in January 2026, reverting to a $200 limit for certain shipments, and industry groups are now pushing for platforms to collect VAT at checkout. Why it matters: another country litigating the same de-minimis/duty-arbitrage question that reshaped US cross-border selling in 2025 and prompted France's fast-fashion levy in September — the low-cost cross-border marketplace model is being contested market by market, not settled once globally.

- **eBay expands "Ship With eBay" managed shipping to more US sellers and categories — while confirming it still charges its Final Value Fee on the shipping cost it collects and passes to the carrier (25–26 Sept).** The programme now covers Collectibles, Fashion, Electronics, Lifestyle, Parts & Accessories, Home & Garden, Business & Industrial and Media, but only for personal (non-business) seller accounts. eBay collects the buyer-paid shipping charge and pays the carrier directly, yet still takes its standard Final Value Fee cut on that shipping amount — an inconsistency sellers have flagged against eBay's own authenticated-sneaker programme, where a flat shipping fee is exempt from Final Value Fees. Why it matters: a live, concrete example of how a marketplace's take-rate design choices (what counts as "commissionable" — goods value only, or goods plus pass-through costs like delivery) shape seller trust and are scrutinised closely once sellers compare programmes side by side.
