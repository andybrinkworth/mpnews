- The DoorDash–Instacart deal is the one to study directly rather than just note: it combines a general-retail marketplace build with a grocery-specific retailer-ordering-and-advertising business, which is the same "marketplace plus ads" bundle Booker is itself assembling — and the regulatory attention it's already drawing over shopper-data concentration and algorithmic pricing/wage-setting is a reminder that this kind of scrutiny is spreading beyond the usual Amazon-sized targets into grocery and retail delivery generally.

- The EU's Union Handling Fee and Product Identifier rule going live (rather than just being announced) is a useful real-world test of a theme already flagged here: complete, standardised product data (GTIN/EAN coverage) is now a hard customs requirement in one of the EU's largest trading relationships, not just an AI-discoverability nicety. Worth another look at how exposed Booker's own supplier catalogue would be if a comparable identifier requirement ever reached UK–EU trade, given Brexit has already created a similar customs-data gap for UK sellers.

- Amazon's insurance mandate landing in the middle of its own stacked peak-season fee calendar is a reminder of how a dominant marketplace can compound seller costs at precisely the moment sellers can least absorb them — worth bearing in mind when timing any changes to Booker's own supplier-facing charges or compliance requirements around a comparable point in the trading calendar.

- Shopify's Q3 results (due today) and any further detail emerging from Amazon Business's Reshape conference remain the most likely near-term sources of substantial new marketplace data and B2B-relevant announcements — flagged again for the next digest.
