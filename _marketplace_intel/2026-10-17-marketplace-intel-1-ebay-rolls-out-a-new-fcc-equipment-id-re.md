---
title: "Marketplace Intel — 1. eBay rolls out a new FCC equipment-ID requirement that can hide non-compliant electronics listings from US buyers, effective 13 October (reported 8 Oct)"
date: 2026-10-17
tags: ["marketplace"]
link: ""
---

Listings for devices that intentionally transmit radio signals (phones, laptops, tablets, routers, drones, Bluetooth devices, wireless cameras, smartwatches, two-way radios, remote-controlled toys) must show a valid FCC ID at the point of sale; sellers who can't confirm one may be unable to create, revise or relist, and non-compliant listings "may be hidden from buyers in the US." This follows an FCC Third Report and Order (first reported in July) that, for the first time, puts compliance obligations directly on marketplaces as well as sellers: a marketplace with physical access to the goods or that takes title is itself liable for displaying a valid, accurate FCC ID, while a marketplace with no physical access or title must display an ID but isn't liable for a seller's inaccuracies if it does reasonable due diligence.

Why it matters: this is a regulator formally splitting compliance liability between platform and seller based on how much control the platform has over the goods - the first concrete rule of its kind tracked in this digest, and a template other product-safety regulators (in the US or elsewhere) could copy for other marketplaces.
