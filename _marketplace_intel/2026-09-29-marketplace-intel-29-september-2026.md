(Covering 26-29 September 2026, since the last digest.)

### TOP 3

1\. Adobe: US online holiday spending forecast to hit $275.1bn this season, up 6.7% year-on-year (28 Sept)

Adobe's holiday forecast (1 Nov-31 Dec) projects Cyber Week growing 7.4% to $47.5bn, with Cyber Monday alone passing $15bn online for the first time (+6.2%) and Black Friday up 9.2% to $12.9bn on electronics and appliances discounting. Adobe also flags a stockpiling trend layered on top of gift-buying: online sales of essentials are set to jump sharply during Cyber Week (personal care +150%, clothing basics +210%, baby products +113%, pet items +93%).

Why it matters: a more bullish, more granular read than the eMarketer holiday forecast already on record (2.6% retail / 6.6% e-commerce growth) - the essentials-stockpiling pattern in particular suggests shoppers are treating online more like a general restocking channel, not just a gift channel, which is worth factoring into Q4 category and delivery-capacity planning.

2\. Temu and Shein face fresh regulatory pushback in Colombia over VAT and de-minimis treatment (28 Sept)

Colombia's Chamber of E-Commerce is pressing the government to close what it calls unfair tax treatment of low-value parcels - a de-minimis exemption designed for occasional shipments that now underwrites an estimated 2.2 million Temu parcels a month. A temporary $50 VAT-exemption threshold was already struck down by Colombia's Constitutional Court in January 2026, reverting to a $200 limit for certain shipments, and industry groups are now pushing for platforms to collect VAT at checkout.

Why it matters: another country litigating the same de-minimis/duty-arbitrage question that reshaped US cross-border selling in 2025 and prompted France's fast-fashion levy in September - the low-cost cross-border marketplace model is being contested market by market, not settled once globally.

3\. eBay expands "Ship With eBay" managed shipping to more US sellers and categories - while confirming it still charges its Final Value Fee on the shipping cost it collects and passes to the carrier (25-26 Sept)

The programme now covers Collectibles, Fashion, Electronics, Lifestyle, Parts & Accessories, Home & Garden, Business & Industrial and Media, but only for personal (non-business) seller accounts. eBay collects the buyer-paid shipping charge and pays the carrier directly, yet still takes its standard Final Value Fee cut on that shipping amount - an inconsistency sellers have flagged against eBay's own authenticated-sneaker programme, where a flat shipping fee is exempt from Final Value Fees.

Why it matters: a live, concrete example of how a marketplace's take-rate design choices (what counts as "commissionable" - goods value only, or goods plus pass-through costs like delivery) shape seller trust and are scrutinised closely once sellers compare programmes side by side.

### WIDER ROUNDUP

- Vinted and Depop's shipping price war kept escalating. Vinted raised its own shipping rates in late September after months of near-giveaway pricing - confirming on 25 Sept variable rates from $0.49-$1.49 (SpeedX) and $0.99-$3.19 (USPS/FedEx) - while Depop ran a $0.99 shipping promotion (from 16 Sept) and free shipping on first orders through 27 Sept. Both platforms have undercut each other on delivery pricing since March 2026 as they fight for lower-value secondhand-fashion buyers; Vinted holds an estimated 50-60% of the UK C2C apparel market against a declining Depop.

- A second Depop "drip pricing" class action is working through the US courts. Filed 15 September in the Northern District of Illinois and still being reported into this week, it alleges Depop's Marketplace Fee (up to 5% of price plus $1) is disclosed only after checkout has begun, following a similar dismissed suit earlier this year.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the EU Commission's Amazon price-parity probe, the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, or the France fast-fashion levy - those remain as previously recorded.

### RELEVANT TO A COMMISSION-MODEL B2B MARKETPLACE

- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window - nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 26-29 September window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- eBay's shipping-fee inconsistency (Final Value Fees charged on pass-through delivery costs in one programme but not another) is the most transferable point this week: it's a concrete illustration of how commission scope - goods value only, versus goods plus delivery/logistics recharges - needs to be applied consistently across a marketplace's own programmes, or it becomes a visible trust issue once sellers compare notes.

- The Depop drip-pricing litigation is a reminder to keep commission and any delivery/service charges visible early in the buyer journey rather than surfaced only at checkout - relevant wherever Booker Marketplace's own pricing stack adds charges on top of a supplier's headline price.

- Temu/Shein's latest regulatory friction, this time in Colombia, is lower-relevance for a domestic B2B platform but useful macro context: de-minimis and duty-arbitrage fights are now recurring in multiple jurisdictions, a sign that cross-border cost structures for any marketplace remain politically unsettled.

- Adobe's more granular holiday forecast (versus the softer eMarketer figure already on record) is a useful input for Q4 commission revenue planning, and the stockpiling-of-essentials trend it flags is worth a look for category and delivery-capacity assumptions specifically.
