Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news stayed thin again this window — nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up, so that section stays short rather than padded.*

**Top 3**

- **A federal judge certified a class of Walmart and eBay shoppers suing Amazon over alleged price inflation — a long-running case missed by the last two digests (ruling issued 9 October 2026, made public and reported from 2 November, with the judge rejecting Amazon's objections confirmed as of 8 November).** The case, Frame-Wilson et al. v. Amazon.com Inc. (filed 2020, W.D. Washington, Judge John H. Chun), alleges Amazon's anti-discounting and price-parity policies — including the algorithm that disqualifies a listing from Amazon's Buy Box if the same product is priced lower elsewhere — pushed up prices for products that third-party sellers also listed on eBay or Walmart Marketplace. The certified class covers US buyers who, since March 2016, made at least four qualifying purchases on eBay or two on Walmart Marketplace of new physical products also monitored through Amazon's competitive-pricing systems; it is narrower than the plaintiffs' original estimate of at least 10 million consumers, and Amazon has asked the Ninth Circuit for permission to appeal. This is certification only — it does not decide liability or find Amazon broke the law. *Why it matters: this is a different, consumer-class route to the same question already being tested by the EU's DMA price-parity probe and other scrutiny of Amazon's ranking rules — whether a marketplace's "don't sell cheaper elsewhere" policies illegally inflate prices on rival platforms. It's a reminder that this kind of price-parity enforcement is being tested through private litigation as well as regulators, and is relevant to any marketplace with its own rules on where else a seller may price lower.*

- **eBay is building a pickup-and-drop-off (PUDO) shipping network to serve both eBay and Depop, and the job postings reveal it partly as a response to GameStop's rejected takeover bid (reported 9 November).** eBay is hiring a Head of PUDO Network, Operations and Partners (San Jose) and a Manager, PUDO Pricing & Monetization (Toronto); its own listing says the US "largely does not exist today" as a physical network of this kind. The move follows CEO Jamie Iannone's 28 October earnings-call announcement of a limited seller drop-off test in several large US metros this quarter, building on eBay's existing UK parcel-shop and locker network, where around 95% of buyers already live within a mile of a participating location. Notably, the article tying this together points out that GameStop CEO Ryan Cohen's rejected $56bn bid for eBay (May 2026) proposed using roughly 1,600 US GameStop stores as drop-off and fulfilment sites — eBay's board turned the bid down, but is now building a version of the same idea itself, through third-party partners rather than GameStop. *Why it matters: a marketplace's own delivery economics increasingly depend on dense, low-cost physical drop-off points, and eBay is choosing to build that network itself after rejecting a ready-made one — worth watching both as a logistics-cost lever and as a case study in how an activist shareholder's idea can outlive the bid that proposed it.*

- **ThredUp's CEO called rival resale marketplaces' marketing and shipping-subsidy spending "wholly irrational" on its Q3 earnings call, as Vinted and Depop keep gaining share (reported 7 November).** CEO James Reinhart estimated peer-to-peer resale rivals could spend up to $500m on marketing and order subsidies in the second half of 2026 alone, on top of separate shipping subsidies; a cited Citizens estimate puts Depop's 2026 marketing spend above $250m, more than double 2025's $119m, and eBay's own Q3 filing showed quarterly marketing costs up $120m year-on-year, mostly tied to Depop and eBay Live. ThredUp itself posted Q3 revenue of $88.2m (+7%), GMV up 20% and a record 1.82 million active buyers (+16%), but average price per item fell from $21 to $18 and net loss widened to $5.5m; Q4 guidance implies a roughly 2% revenue decline and negative adjusted EBITDA, with the CEO putting the competitive hit from rivals at about $5m of revenue. Shipping-price moves cited include Vinted's shift from free to $0.99 (August) to variable $0.49–$1.49 rates (September), and Poshmark cutting its standard rate to $0.99 on 3 November. *Why it matters: a concrete, numbers-backed example of how venture-funded rivals' subsidy spending can squeeze an established commission platform's growth and margin outlook even while its own usage metrics improve — a useful data point on the real cost of fighting for buyers on price and shipping rather than product or service.*

**Wider roundup**

- Etsy told sellers in a 6 November message promoting its Cyber Specials sale to message past buyers through Etsy Conversations about their discounts — advice that appears to conflict with Etsy's own Seller Policy banning unsolicited promotional messages — then retracted it three days later (9 November) as "incorrect," apologising and suggesting shop announcements or social media instead. Etsy hasn't said whether sellers who acted on the original advice will be protected from its own spam-enforcement system, which can auto-mute an account for 48 hours with no support override (reported 9 November).

- TCGPlayer began rolling out free tracking for Marketplace orders of $49.99 or less from 2 November, reaching all sellers by 6 November, using a USPS Intelligent Mail barcode printed on a packing slip inside a standard envelope — but still falls short of eBay's long-standing Standard Envelope service for trading cards (offered since January 2021), which includes integrated postage, tracking and shipping protection with no acceptance scan required (reported 6 November).

- eBay's Winter 2026 seller update touted continued Live-selling growth, expanded AI listing and bulk-listing tools, a new seller financing partnership with Fundbox, and more control over Promoted Offsite ads (sellers can now choose automatic or manual optimisation for off-platform exposure on sites like Google and Facebook, though still without CPC bidding or budget control) — rounded up this week alongside the other items below.

- eBay's managed-balance payout system (eBay Balance) became mandatory for enrolled US sellers from 4 November, resetting payout schedules and how sellers access and withdraw funds.

- Poshmark cut its standard US shipping rate to $0.99 with no stated end date from 3 November, and is offering free shipping on Posh LIVE orders of $75 or more — while sellers reported payout withdrawal failures returning this week, weeks after Poshmark attributed earlier delays to a banking-partner transition.

- Whatnot stopped covering USPS weight-adjustment surcharges from 4 November; US sellers now pay the extra postage themselves when a package weighs more than its label declared.

- eBay flagged a further USPS Ground Advantage service change taking effect 8 November, without detailing whether it raises or lowers costs — available reporting hadn't clarified the terms as this digest was compiled.

- Catching up on two items missed by the last two digests: the FTC sent information-only orders on 28 October to nine companies — DoorDash, Instacart, Walmart, Target, Uber, Lyft and three airlines — as part of a study into whether they use personalised data to set prices or gig-worker pay; it is not an enforcement action and the order landed the day before DoorDash's agreed Instacart acquisition was announced. Separately, Depop ended independent shipping for new US listings from 28 October, requiring sellers to use Depop Shipping via USPS; CFO Peggy Alford has said Depop's lower take rate partly reflects shipping subsidies being booked as revenue reductions.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the FTC's separate Amazon monopolisation case (still on its 29 March 2027 trial date), the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, the EU Commission's Amazon price-parity probe, or France's fast-fashion levy — those all remain as previously recorded. Texas's proposed repeal of its marketplace-fee tax (Rule 3.330) remains in its public-comment period, which closes 15 November.

**B2B relevance for Booker Marketplace**

- eBay's PUDO build-out is the one to study directly: its own job postings admit the US lacks the dense physical drop-off network it needs, and the plan is pointedly similar to the drop-off/fulfilment use of retail stores that GameStop's Ryan Cohen proposed in his rejected takeover bid. Booker's own depot network is a comparable physical asset — worth asking whether it could double as pickup, drop-off or returns infrastructure for Booker Marketplace rather than being used purely for stock distribution.

- ThredUp's "wholly irrational" complaint about rival marketing and shipping-subsidy spending is a useful cautionary data point, with real numbers behind it: a venture-funded competitor spending heavily on acquisition and shipping subsidies can visibly squeeze an established platform's growth and margin guidance even while its own usage metrics improve. Worth bearing in mind if a subsidy-funded entrant ever targets Booker's buyer or supplier base on price or delivery cost alone.

- Etsy's two-day reversal on messaging advice — telling sellers to do something that conflicts with its own published policy, then retracting it — is a governance lesson this digest keeps returning to. Any supplier-facing guidance Booker sends out is worth checking against its own stated policies before it goes out, since sellers who follow official advice in good faith can still be caught by automated enforcement.

- eBay making its managed-balance payout system mandatory, paired with a new seller-financing tie-up (Fundbox), shows a marketplace embedding payments and working-capital products directly into the seller relationship rather than leaving it to outside banks — a stickiness and monetisation lever worth watching as a direction for Booker's own supplier relationships.

- The FTC's personalised-pricing information requests to Walmart, Target and two delivery marketplaces are a reminder that dynamic or personalised pricing based on customer data is drawing regulatory attention before any wrongdoing is even alleged — relevant background if Booker's own pricing logic ever varies by buyer-specific data.
