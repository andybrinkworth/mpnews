### WIDER ROUNDUP

- StockX Live, the platform's live-auction format, expanded into NBA, NFL and MLB trading cards on 8 October, with a four-day launch event through 11 October. New rules ban random-assignment "card break" tools (wheels, dice, RNGs) in favour of disclosed, buyer-chosen distribution, require sellers to film breaks on camera, and limit repacks to sellers with at least six months of verifiable sourcing history - StockX is positioning Live as a direct challenger to eBay Live and Whatnot, the latter of which faces separate arbitration claims alleging some break/repack activity amounts to illegal gambling.

- eBay Australia will make its Free Selling cash balance spendable on the platform from 3 November (announced 2 Oct) - sellers can use held proceeds to buy items, pay for shipping labels or cover fees, with spending capped at AU$2,000 per transaction/day and AU$5,000 over a rolling 30 days; the balance earns no interest and isn't covered by Australia's Financial Claims Scheme, instead backed by a JPMorgan Chase bank guarantee. It mirrors the "spendable balance" model eBay has already rolled out in the US and UK.

- Final, independently-tallied data on Amazon's 6-7 October UK/US Prime Big Deal Days (Numerator/Adobe, reported 8 Oct, missed by the last entry) showed US household spend up 9% year-on-year to $114.01, with Amazon's spending lift nearly four times the size of Walmart's competing Fall Deals event over the same two days - a useful benchmark for comparing marketplace promotional pull ahead of peak season.

- In South Africa, the Southern African Clothing and Textile Workers' Union won backing from the Cosatu trade union federation (15 Oct) for a government ban on Shein and Temu, citing an estimated 34,000 textile-sector jobs at risk; an independent economist quoted in the same report called the figure likely outdated since it predates 2025 tax changes that have already slowed both platforms' South African growth sharply, and warned an outright ban could push trade underground rather than fix it.

- No new dated developments surfaced this window in the FTC v Amazon ad-auction pricing suit, the EU DMA Meta/Facebook Marketplace gatekeeper case, the EU Commission's Amazon price-parity probe, France's fast-fashion levy, the EU's confirmed 1 November Union Handling Fee, or the EU's mandatory customs Product Identifier rule - those all remain as previously recorded. Amazon Business's "Reshape 2026" conference (27-28 October, Nashville) remains the most likely near-term source of genuine B2B marketplace news; nothing new has come out of it yet.

### RELEVANT TO A COMMISSION-MODEL B2B MARKETPLACE

- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin again this window - nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 14-16 October window, so this stays short by design rather than padded. (One piece of UK-adjacent coverage found this week was explicitly speculative - a trade-press forecast that Costco UK will likely join an on-demand delivery marketplace like Uber Eats or Deliveroo by March 2027 - and is flagged here only as a thing to watch for, not as news that's happened.)

- The Mirakl/NVIDIA item is the one to actually act on, not just note: it's the clearest sign yet that AI-assisted catalogue/product-data onboarding is moving from Mirakl roadmap talk into a claimed, shipping capability. Worth finding out directly from Mirakl whether and when faster onboarding tooling becomes available on Booker Marketplace's own instance, particularly with supplier onboarding volume likely to rise ahead of peak trading.

- Texas's move to reclassify marketplace fees as commissions rather than taxable services is a reminder that the tax treatment of a marketplace's own take-rate is not settled or uniform even within one country, let alone across jurisdictions - worth keeping half an eye on in case a similar question (is a marketplace commission a service fee, a disbursement, or something else) ever becomes live for HMRC or equivalent UK treatment of Booker's own supplier charges.

- StockX Live's card-break disclosure rules (and the parallel gambling-related legal exposure Whatnot is fighting over the same mechanic) are a transferable governance lesson: any new promotional or gamified selling format a marketplace adds needs clear, written seller disclosure rules before launch, not after a dispute - relevant if Booker Marketplace ever experiments with bundled, auction-style or promotional selling formats.

- The Amazon FTC monopolisation trial holding its March 2027 date, and the ad-auction suit running in parallel, are now a steady background condition rather than a new risk - worth noting mainly because it continues to shape how aggressively sellers diversify away from relying on any single dominant marketplace.
