- **eBay moves fee-free sellers in Australia into managed shipping, following its UK and US playbook (announced 9 Oct, phased rollout from 11 Nov).** Under a User Agreement update, eBay will calculate shipping cost from the buyer's location and parcel details, collect postage directly from buyers, and issue prepaid Australia Post labels for eligible domestic sales by sellers without a Pro plan; sellers offering "free shipping" instead pay a discounted flat rate out of their proceeds. Sellers lose control over carrier choice and can't cancel or replace a prepaid label without risking delivery protections; keeping full control over shipping now requires upgrading to a Pro plan. Why it matters: this is the third market in a year — after the UK's Simple Delivery mandate and the US "Ship With eBay" expansion, both covered in the last two digests — where eBay has traded seller choice over shipping for its own cut of the logistics flow. UK sellers reportedly saw rates rise once moved onto managed shipping, so Australian sellers may see the same.

- **The EU's mandatory customs Product Identifier (PID) data requirement, taking effect 1 November, is complicating marketplace sales of used, vintage and one-of-a-kind goods (reported from 5 Oct, continuing coverage into this window).** From 1 November, anyone shipping into the EU from outside it must supply a Merchant Product Identifier, a standardised code (GTIN/EAN/UPC/ISBN) where one exists, and otherwise a manufacturer-assigned Non-Standardised Product Identifier — regardless of parcel value, and across all customs declaration types, not just low-value ones. eBay says it will supply the Merchant Product Identifier itself but has not said what sellers should enter when no manufacturer-level identifier exists at all, which is common for older clothing, vintage collectibles, worn vehicle parts and handmade goods. Why it matters: this is a second, distinct EU compliance layer landing on the same date as the already-confirmed €2 Union Handling Fee — and unlike a per-item fee, missing product-identifier data can delay or block customs clearance outright, a sharper risk for any recommerce or long-tail marketplace than a cost increase would be.

- **Poshmark ends its subsidy of the USPS hazardous-materials noncompliance fee, shifting the full $50 charge back onto sellers from 19 October (reported 6 Oct).** Sellers had been paying a reduced $5 since 1 August while Poshmark covered the remaining $45; from 19 October a flagged shipment costs the seller the full $50. A common trigger is leftover hazardous-materials markings (such as the "Limited Quantity" diamond) left on reused packaging. Mercari, by contrast, still splits the same USPS fee with sellers; eBay and Etsy offer no equivalent protection at all. Why it matters: another example — after eBay's unannounced currency-conversion fee rise two digests ago — of a marketplace quietly moving an operational cost back onto sellers via a policy update rather than a headline fee change, at a moment when several platforms are already raising seller costs ahead of peak season.
