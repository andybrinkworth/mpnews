- **Texas moves to repeal the sales tax on marketplace seller fees it only started collecting a year ago (executive order 30 Sept; proposed rule due to publish in the Texas Register today, 16 Oct).** Texas Comptroller Don Huffines signed an executive order on 30 September directing his office to amend Rule 3.330, which since October 2025 has classified certain marketplace seller fees — the commission Amazon, eBay, Etsy, Poshmark and others charge for listing, offering or facilitating a sale — as taxable "data processing" services. The proposed amendment, due for publication in the Texas Register on 16 October with a public-comment deadline of 15 November, would state plainly that marketplace fees are not data processing or information services. Huffines calls the existing tax a hidden, voter-unapproved burden on small sellers; his predecessor had defended it as applying existing law to two separate taxable transactions (the retail sale, and the seller's service). Sellers must keep paying the tax under the current rule until the process concludes, and it's unclear whether any refund would be retroactive. Why it matters: this is a regulator moving in the opposite direction from most of what this digest tracks — reclassifying a marketplace's take-rate as a commission rather than a taxable service, rather than adding a new cost. It's a reminder that how a jurisdiction defines "the marketplace fee" for tax purposes is still unsettled and contestable, not a fixed category — worth knowing given how much of this digest's running thread (France's levy, the EU's Union Handling Fee, Colombia's VAT fight) has gone the other way.

- **Mirakl says it has used NVIDIA GPUs and open AI models to cut seller catalogue onboarding to under two hours, across more than 70 million products (NVIDIA blog, 12 Oct; reported 13 Oct).** The claim appeared in an NVIDIA roundup of French companies building on its hardware, published nine days ahead of Jensen Huang's GTC Berlin keynote. NVIDIA says Mirakl has onboarded over 70 million seller products using fine-tuned and open models running on its GPUs and inference software, cutting catalogue onboarding time to "under two hours." No baseline onboarding time, processing period or model names were given, so the scale of the improvement can't be independently checked — every figure in the post comes from NVIDIA or its customers. Mirakl's platform underpins more than 450 marketplaces and 100,000+ third-party sellers; faster, cleaner product-data ingestion also feeds Mirakl Ads, its advertising business, since sponsored placement needs structured catalogue data first. Why it matters: this is the platform Booker Marketplace itself is built on, making it the most directly relevant item this digest has carried in weeks rather than an analogy. If faster, AI-assisted catalogue onboarding reaches Booker's own Mirakl instance, it could shorten the time between a new supplier joining and their full range being live and sponsorable — worth a direct question to the Mirakl account team on timing and availability, independent of NVIDIA's own figures.

- **A federal judge keeps the FTC's monopolisation case against Amazon on its March 2027 trial date, rejecting Amazon's bid to push it to June 2027 (ruling reported 9 Oct).** US District Judge John Chun denied Amazon's request to delay the trial, saying he has "been preparing for some time" for the existing 29 March 2027 date and saw no good reason to disturb it. This is separate from the FTC's ad-auction pricing suit (filed September, also still live) and was missed by the last two entries despite falling inside their stated windows. Why it matters: sustained antitrust and regulatory pressure on the dominant marketplace continues on schedule rather than slipping further — this digest has now tracked two separate live federal cases against Amazon (ad-auction pricing, and this monopolisation case) running in parallel through 2026 and into 2027, a backdrop worth keeping in view for how aggressively any marketplace should rely on an Amazon-style playbook.
