- **eBay is cutting about 9% of its global workforce — its second layoff round in 2026 (CEO memo 20 Oct, reported 21–22 Oct).** CEO Jamie Iannone told staff the company is "reducing our workforce by approximately 9%" to simplify operations and redirect spending toward higher-growth areas — C2C selling, AI, eBay Live and Vehicles. A WARN notice shows 229 roles cut at eBay's San Jose HQ alone, spanning engineering, product, design and data science; unverified reports from a Chinese outlet claim far deeper cuts to eBay's China R&D operation, which eBay disputes. This follows roughly 800 roles (6%) cut in February, and comes after eBay closed its ~$1.4bn Depop acquisition in July and continued shifting development work to India. eBay expects $130–150m in pre-tax severance charges, mostly in Q4. The cuts land against a backdrop of GameStop's Ryan Cohen holding eBay's second-largest shareholding after his rejected $56bn takeover bid in May, though eBay hasn't said whether that pressure influenced the decision; shares were already trading near record highs before the announcement. eBay reports Q3 earnings on 28 October. Why it matters: a second major round of cuts at a marketplace that just posted 15% revenue growth signals eBay is choosing margin discipline and reinvestment concentration over headcount even while growing — worth watching for what "higher-growth area" prioritisation looks like when it's your own commission marketplace deciding where to spend.

- **Etsy bans AI-generated patterns, recipes and instruction content outright, and tightens listing-photo authenticity rules, with no grace period for existing listings (announced 22 Oct).** AI-generated knitting/crochet/sewing patterns, recipes, woodworking plans and other how-to content are now banned outright, alongside bot-generated or mass-automated listings made without real human creative input. Most listings now need at least one real, unedited photo of the actual item on a real background — cropping, resizing, lighting/exposure changes and background swaps are no longer allowed on that core image — and pattern/recipe/seed listings specifically need a real photo of a finished result. Sellers must disclose when AI created the product itself (not just an edited photo). Etsy says it's rolling out AI-image detection prompts on the listing form and will expand removals over coming months; repeat violations can mean shop removal. Why it matters: this is the clearest, most enforced marketplace policy yet on where AI is and isn't allowed in a listing — a concrete template for how any marketplace might draw the line between "AI helped make this" and "this is not what you'll actually receive," right as catalogue data quality becomes an AI-discoverability requirement industry-wide (a thread this digest flagged back in September).

- **Meta's contested AI-training terms remain unresolved just days before their 30 October effective date (ongoing coverage, 16–18 Oct, carried into this window).** Since the last digest flagged these terms taking effect with no advertiser opt-out, reporting has got messier rather than clearer: on 17 October The Verge said the disputed "train" language had been removed from Meta's commercial terms page, with Meta saying the crawling terms only ever applied to ads/business products — but a capture of Meta's own UK terms preview taken the next day, 18 October, still contained the original training language unchanged, with no ads-only limitation. No reporting since 18 October has resolved which version will actually govern from 30 October. Why it matters: with the effective date now just three days away, this has gone from "a platform wrote broad AI-training language into terms no one can negotiate" to "even outside reporters can't confirm what the binding terms will say" — a worse outcome for trust than the original story, and a reminder of exactly the kind of ambiguity any marketplace should avoid when it eventually publishes its own supplier/buyer data-and-AI policy.
