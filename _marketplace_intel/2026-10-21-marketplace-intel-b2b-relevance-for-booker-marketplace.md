- Genuinely new, dated UK wholesale/B2B marketplace competitor news was thin yet again this window — nothing dated from Bestway, Nisa, Musgrave, Costco UK or Amazon Business UK turned up in the 9–13 October window, so this stays short by design rather than padded.

- The EU's mandatory PID customs rule is the most transferable point this week: it's a regulator moving from value-based thresholds (de minimis, the Union Handling Fee) to product-data-based ones, where the consequence of a gap isn't a fee but a blocked shipment. Worth a look at how complete Booker Marketplace's own supplier product data (GTIN/EAN coverage, manufacturer identifiers) would be if a similar requirement reached UK–EU trade, given Brexit has already created a comparable customs-data gap for UK sellers.

- eBay's Australian managed-shipping mandate is a third instance of a trade-off already tracked twice this year: a marketplace gaining control over shipping and its margin at the cost of seller choice. Worth keeping as a reference point for how far Booker would want to standardise shipping/logistics across its own supplier base versus leaving it to individual suppliers.

- Poshmark's fee-shift is the same governance pattern as eBay's unannounced currency-conversion rise: marketplaces moving operational costs onto sellers through a quiet policy or terms-of-service update rather than a clear fee change. Worth bearing in mind for how Booker communicates any changes to supplier-side charges, so trust isn't eroded by suppliers discovering changes after the fact.

- Amazon Business's Reshape agenda (AI and agents, procurement strategy, controls and compliance, supply-chain resilience) is a useful preview of what the largest B2B marketplace considers its current priorities — worth watching for concrete announcements in the next digest once the conference runs 27–28 October.
